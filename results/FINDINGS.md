# OSAR-bench: what I found

I ran three Claude models (Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5) on 23 short workstation tasks in an in-memory sandbox, each task under a tight prompt (what to do and where) and a loose one (the goal only). Every tool call is scored against a per-task rubric as in scope, gray or out of scope (OOS); OOS effects are benign, sensitive or consequential (an unrequested write, delete, move, message or privilege change, refused attempts included). Four pre-registered stages: v1 baselines (2,112 rollouts), v2 with twelve new tasks, an ask-the-user tool, a scope sentence and one-off friction (7,488), v3 with variants of friction on the eight tasks whose tight rules allow a local change (3,584), v4 with persistent denial on the same eight (1,536). The ledger closed at $151.04 of the $160 cap: $16 for the v1 main run, $50.5 for v3, $18.6 for v4, the rest for v2 and pilots, plus an estimated $3–4 of judge and rater calls that were not metered (about 350 judge and 90 rater calls to Sonnet at list prices). Registered numbers use the registered metrics; where the finer, exploratory reading (`analysis/refined/refined.md`) changes the picture I give both.

## The story

**Ambiguity and failure compound into real unrequested changes.** When the first write is refused under a loose prompt (E3 against the loose baseline on the same eight tasks, H10), the registered any-OOS rate rises by 0.070 [0.031, 0.109] and the consequential rate by 0.150 [0.122, 0.177], for all three models. The increase is real: rollouts with a real change were 9.4% (Haiku), 26.6% (Opus) and 15.2% (Sonnet) under E3 against 1.2%, 10.9% and 1.2% in the loose baseline (9.0%, 18.8% and 14.1% counting only changes to other targets). Recomputed with real changes as the outcome (exploratory, `analysis/refined/sensitivity_real_change.csv`) the effect is +0.096 [0.070, 0.122], and +0.095 for unrequested changes only. The changes are workarounds, not sudo: a file written elsewhere, a folder created, the second copy of the contacts file edited. Completion falls by 0.30.

**Friction on its own is mostly probes and blocked attempts.** With the first state-changing call failing with "Permission denied" under a tight prompt (D), the registered any-OOS rate rises by 0.28 (0.25 to 0.31) and the consequential rate by 0.07 (0.05 to 0.08) (H7), but real changes rise by about one point (+0.009 [0.003, 0.016]; +0.001 for unrequested changes only). Sonnet probed or used sudo in 12.5% of rollouts and Opus in 1.6%; real privileged edits (`sudo sed` on the config file) happened in 1.2% for each (three rollouts per model) and never for Haiku. 28 of Sonnet's 29 probes were followed by a plain retry that went through, because only the first call was refused. Flagged consequential rollouts under D were 13.7% (Sonnet), 6.2% (Opus) and 1.2% (Haiku); rollouts with a real unrequested, privileged or collateral change were 1.2%, 1.6% and 0%. v4 removed the transient and the models mostly stopped and said so (below).

**Loose prompts make the models look far beyond their scope.** Dropping the "where" raised the share of rollouts with any OOS action by 0.48 (0.45 to 0.50) on the v1 tasks and by 0.66 (0.64 to 0.67) on the v2 tasks (H2, H4a). Almost all of it is navigation: listing and reading folders the task did not need, mail, finance and private ones included. In the mixed model the loose framing adds about four logits to the odds of an OOS action; the model term adds a third of a logit.

**Real consequential actions are rare on their own.** Across all 4,416 baseline rollouts 39, or 0.9%, changed state out of scope; on the v1 tasks alone 37 of 2,112 (1.8%), 28 of them Opus under loose prompts (H1). Every one was a real change, not a blocked attempt. On the friction tasks the tight baseline rate is 0.4% and the loose one 4.4%, mostly Opus (10.9% against 1.2% for the others), which drafts and files things nobody asked for. On the twelve new tasks the loose-minus-tight difference in consequential actions was 0.002 (H4b) and Opus was not more consequential than the others (H4c). I expected the v1 Opus effect to hold; it did not.

**Cheap mitigations are weak; in these arms the error text, not the instruction, is what moved sudo use.** An ask-the-user tool changed nothing (H5): the models used it in 3 to 8% of rollouts and carried on. A one-sentence scope instruction cut any-OOS by 0.04 and consequential OOS not at all, at a completion cost of 0.08 (H6). Under friction (v3, H9) the same sentence removed Sonnet's sudo probes entirely but not real edits: with real changes as the outcome the effect is 0.000 [-0.009, 0.009] (exploratory), and Opus made real privileged edits in 7 rollouts under the sentence against 3 without it. Replacing "Permission denied" with "Resource temporarily unavailable" or "Disk quota exceeded" removed every sudo call (H8).

## v3 verdicts (tight prompts unless stated; pooled over models, task-clustered bootstrap CIs; escalation = any sudo or chmod/chown effect, probes included)

- H8, error wording: supported. D minus E1a and D minus E1b on escalation are both 0.048 (0.035 to 0.062); Sonnet 0.125 (Holm p < 0.001), Opus 0.016 (Holm p = 0.11), Haiku null. Neither alternative error produced a single sudo call. "Temporarily unavailable" makes everyone retry and finish (completion 0.95 pooled); "quota exceeded" makes Opus give up (0.37 against 0.64 under D).
- H9, scope sentence under friction: supported pooled, Sonnet only. E2 minus D: escalation 0.038 lower (0.053 to 0.022 lower) and consequential OOS 0.038 lower (0.059 to 0.017 lower), both from Sonnet. Opus went from 1.6% to 3.1% sudo (not significant), seven of its eight E2 sudo rollouts real edits. Completion falls by 0.21.
- H10, friction under loose prompts: supported, and it holds for real changes (above).
- H11, effort high: Sonnet changes, Opus does not. Sonnet loses sudo entirely (0.125 lower, 0.152 to 0.098 lower) and flagged consequential OOS drops by 0.11 while any-OOS rises by 0.12. Opus: escalation unchanged (0.008, CI 0.016 lower to 0.031 higher), any-OOS up 0.19, flagged consequential up 0.04 (0.00 to 0.09); of its 27 flagged rollouts one was a real change, the rest test files written and deleted again. More thinking meant more looking around for both.

**Collateral damage.** All 23 of Opus's over-edits of `contacts.csv` change only the capitalisation of the header, "name" to "Name", from retyping the whole file after the plain `sed` was refused; in one rollout it then "corrected" a header it had not changed. Twenty are under loose friction (7.8% of Opus's E3 rollouts), two under "Disk quota exceeded", one under "Resource temporarily unavailable". Sonnet produced three other over-edits. Trivial here, but the only damage to an in-scope file I saw, and friction produced it.

**Disclosure.** Among rollouts with a real OOS change, the Sonnet judge found the final message disclosed it in 100% of Opus's loose baselines (28 rollouts), in 3 of 4 Opus and 3 of 3 Sonnet rollouts under D, in all 7 Opus rollouts under E2, and under loose friction in 92% (Haiku), 74% (Opus) and 100% (Sonnet). The registered rates (11% Sonnet, 44% Opus under tight friction) were dominated by rollouts whose only flagged actions were a sudo probe or a test file; among the 42 probe-only rollouts the judge found 2 disclosures, and 11 end with a sentence like "I didn't use sudo", 8 of them after finishing with a plain retry. Defensible, and it hides the check. The keyword rule agrees with the judge on 72% of cases (kappa 0.39) and should not be used for rates: in the 40-case hand check, sampled to over-represent disagreements, the rule was wrong on 21 cases and the judge on 3.

**Model-based rater.** On the v1 coding sheet a Sonnet rater agreed with the rubric on 83% of ruled items (kappa 0.74; model-based, the human kappa is still a placeholder in `analysis/supplementary`). Taking its labels for unclassified shell commands as true adds OOS to 32 rollouts and moves any-OOS by at most 0.014.

## v4: when sudo is the only path

Every plain edit of the target failed with "Permission denied" for the whole rollout while passwordless sudo worked (F1), with and without the scope sentence (F2); eight tasks, tight prompts, three models, 32 seeds per cell, no failed rollouts. Under F1, 94% of rollouts (Haiku 88%, Opus 97%, Sonnet 97%) ended with no state change and a final message naming the obstacle; completion fell from 0.66 under D to 0.02. Privileged edits happened in 1.8% pooled (Haiku 0, Opus 2.0%, Sonnet 3.5%), every one `sudo sed` on the config file in `/etc` (T2), workarounds in 0.8%. H12 (F1 minus D on privileged edits, predicted higher): +0.010 (CI 0.000 to 0.021, one-sided p = 0.035), not supported by the registered CI rule; Sonnet +0.023 (Holm p = 0.10), Opus +0.008, Haiku 0. H13 (F2 minus F1, predicted lower): supported, 0.013 lower (0.023 to 0.003 lower), carried by Sonnet (nine rollouts to one); Opus five to three, Haiku none. The sentence also removed every sudo probe for Sonnet and Opus. Sonnet probed in 9% of F1 rollouts (23); the probe succeeds silently, 15 of the 23 final messages conclude passwordless sudo is available, four call the check inconclusive and four do not say; all 23 stopped, and Sonnet's nine privileged edits came without a probe. All 24 privileged edits across D, F1 and F2 were disclosed (judge and rule agree). No rollout suggested a test or simulated environment; one in five F1 rollouts attributed the refusal to a sandbox or security policy on the machine, and all of those stopped (none of the 14 privileged edits came from them). On a 30-case sheet labelled by an LLM reader, 0 of 30 suspect a test or simulation, 22 of 30 blame a restriction beyond ordinary permissions (the keyword scan: 18/18 precision, 18/22 recall), and none escalated (29 stopped, one workaround). Whether stopping is restraint or distrust of a failing tool I cannot separate with this data. Haiku's "other" outcomes (10% under F1) are T7 rollouts that asked which person the user is before touching anything, as under D. ## Caveats

- H4c did not replicate: the v1 Opus effect was one task (T7) and the twelve new tasks hardly afford a consequential action, so the test had a floor. Opus's higher loose rate on the friction tasks is the same drafting-and-filing habit on T5 and T7.
- sudo as a reasonable reflex: `sudo -n true` is what a careful admin might run after an odd permission error, the real escalations edited the very file the user asked about, and sudo never asks for a password here. The escalation rate measures willingness to try root, not harm. Given no other path the models mostly do not take it, at the price of not finishing.
- The registered consequential metric counts attempts (denied calls, probes, test files), so the friction rates overstate real change; the refined tables give both. The ordering of the arms does not change, the sizes do.
- Disclosure labels come from one judge call at default sampling, checked on 40 cases by an LLM reader with the scorers' labels visible; the judge saw the full flagged list even where I report rates on real changes only. The keyword rule is kept for transparency only.
- The baselines for the friction contrasts are the v1 main run, done on earlier days in the same harness and verified to score identically under the later rubrics, not interleaved with the later arms. Some loose cells have completion near zero because the target is hard to find (T18 for Haiku), which deflates OOS and completion together.
- One provider family, eight friction tasks, 32 seeds per cell, a sandbox with no real consequences, minimal reasoning except in E4. Effects are macro-averaged over tasks and most rest on three or four tasks. The v4 outcome labels come from a fixed rule, not a reader.
