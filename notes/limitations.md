# Limitations and open points (notes to self)

What I know is weak in the study and what I did about each point. Numbers are the ones in the paper.

Rubric. I wrote the allow and gray lists myself. They were frozen before each stage's data (branches prereg-v1 to prereg-v4, hashes in prereg/HASHES.txt), but they are still my calls. The registered consequential metric counts refused attempts, sudo probes and test files. Under one-off denial that is most of the effect: flagged 13.7% against real 1.2% for Sonnet, 6.2% against 1.6% for Opus. So the confirmatory story for H7 and H9 is about attempts. I added the probe/exec and unrequested/privileged/collateral split (osar/categories.py, Table 3) and a sensitivity table (Appendix C): H10 holds (+0.096 for real changes), H7 shrinks to +0.009, H9 goes to 0.000. The split was defined after I had seen the v3 data, so it stays exploratory. The 300-item packet (prereg/coding_sheet_v4.csv) was rated by three model raters, not humans; see below. Because their majority missed 0.70, I applied the registered consequence: rubric v5 (prereg/RUBRIC_v5_REVISION.md, rules fixed and committed before any re-scoring), every rollout re-scored, both versions reported (results/analysis_v5/comparison_v4_v5.md). v5 makes non-sensitive listings, stats and reads without a task rule gray, the earlier T5 email in scope, the home listing under loose prompts gray, and read-only unclassified commands gray. It changes two H3 verdicts and shrinks the any-OOS effects of H2 (0.475 to 0.367), H4a (0.656 to 0.421), H7 (0.277 to 0.065) and H10 (0.070 to 0.049); nothing about consequential actions moves.

Toy shell, passwordless sudo. The sandbox is small and deterministic, and the models can tell something is off. Their messages mention inconsistent output, a shell that refuses loops and variables, and a sudo check that prints nothing. Whole-call rollback and a bare "error: Permission denied" without an exit status are not how a real shell behaves. So "stop and report" under persistent denial may be distrust of a broken tool rather than restraint about root. What I checked: a fixed keyword scan over all 2,304 D, F1 and F2 rollouts. No message uses "simulated", "mock", "fake", "synthetic", "test environment", "being tested", "evaluation", "benchmark" or "not real". "sandbox" appears in 2 to 4% of D, 18 to 34% of F1 and 8 to 25% of F2 rollouts, always as a guessed cause of the error on the user's machine. Under F1 the 182 rollouts that name a sandbox all stopped and none escalated; the other 586 stopped in 92% and hold all 14 privileged edits. That fits both readings. Finding (d) is worded "stopped and reported" and the paper says restraint and distrust cannot be separated. The 30-case sheet (prereg/suspicion_handcheck.csv) was labelled by an LLM reader: 0 of 30 suspect a test or simulation, 22 of 30 blame a restriction beyond ordinary permissions, the keyword scan has 18/18 precision and 18/22 recall on it, and none of the 30 escalated (29 stopped and reported, one workaround). Open: a judge scoring over all rollouts, and a replication in a container with a real shell and a real sudo.

Power at rare rates. Rates of 1 to 4% are 3 to 10 rollouts per cell. At one-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power, 256 per arm detects about 3.8 points from a 1.2% baseline, 768 pooled about 1.6 points, 384 per cell about 1.9 points from 0.2%. H12 saw +1.0 point with the CI lower bound at exactly 0.000 and p = 0.035. By the registered rule that is not supported. It is a small effect the test could not count, not a null. The paper says so (Section 4.4 and the Limitations).

H4c. The twelve v2 tasks are read-only or single-answer chores. Consequential actions were nearly impossible on them by construction, so H4b and H4c had a floor. The v1 Opus effect was one task, T7, with 7 of 32 loose rollouts emailing colleagues. "Not replicated on tasks that do not afford it" is the honest wording and that is what the Limitations say. Open: write-tasks in the new-task set.

Probe against real sudo. The registered escalation metric lumps sudo -n true with sudo sed. Under D, 29 of Sonnet's 32 sudo rollouts were probes and 28 of those were followed by a plain retry that worked; real edits were 3 per model for Sonnet and Opus, none for Haiku. H8 is first of all an effect on whether sudo is tried; H9 removed probes, not edits; H11 for Sonnet removed sudo entirely. Sections 4.3, 4.5 and 4.6 say this now. Under persistent denial the real rate is 1.8% pooled (14 rollouts in F1, 4 in F2), all sudo sed on the config file.

Collateral against unrequested. All 23 Opus over-edits of contacts.csv are the header changed from "name" to "Name" after a refused sed made it retype the file. They count as consequential. 20 of Opus's 68 real E3 rollouts are this. The 9 to 27% range includes them; without them Opus is 18.8%, so the range for changes to other targets is 9 to 19%. Both ranges are in the abstract and Section 4.3.

Disclosure judge. One Sonnet call per rollout at default sampling (the SDK version does not take a temperature). An LLM reader labelled the 40 hand-check cases with the scorers' labels visible, so it was not blind. The sample over-represents disagreements: the rule was wrong on 21 of 40, the judge on 3, and the 16 agreements were all right; reweighted, about 24% error for the rule and 4% for the judge. The judge saw probes and test files in the action list even where I now report rates on real changes only. The 24 disclosed privileged edits were each judged once. Procedure in Appendix E of the paper.

Model raters. Three blind raters (claude-fable-5-1, claude-opus-5-5, claude-sonnet-5-5) labelled the 300-item sheet from the fixed prompt in prereg/rater_prompt.txt, never seeing the key or each other. Against the scorer: kappa 0.56 (Fable, 226 items), 0.59 (Opus, 299), 0.57 (Sonnet, 300); majority vote 0.59, agreement 0.75; pairwise 0.70 to 0.79; Fleiss' kappa 0.74 on the 226 items all three labelled. The 0.70 threshold was registered for humans and the model majority is below it; the raters agree with each other more than with my rubric. Where they disagree with the rubric is the ordinary stratified sample (65% majority match); T7 emails and privileged edits are 100%. On unclassified commands the raters say gray (24 to 28 of 37) or in_scope, almost never oos. Fable refused 74 items at the API (20 of the 33 privileged edits, 21 unclassified commands, 33 others). Temperature 0 was requested and rejected by the API; all three ran at default sampling. The earlier Sonnet rater on the v1 sheet, with the old guide, had kappa 0.74. Against the v5 scorer the majority reaches kappa 0.68 (0.69 on the same 189 items; per rater 0.75, 0.63, 0.61), still just below 0.70. A model rating model behaviour from the same family is still the main weakness; no human has rated anything.

Anthropic only, low effort. Three Claude 5.5 models from one lab, effort low except E4 on two models. The Grok 4.6 arm from the v1 plan has not been run. The title says "LLM agents"; the Limitations say what the evidence is.

Baselines not interleaved. The baselines for D, E and F were run one to two days before the arms. A model change behind the API in that window would be invisible. Every log records the model string and parameters, but that does not fix the design. Said in the Limitations.

Completion trade-off. Under F1 completion fell from 0.66 to 0.02. Stopping and reporting is not free. Said in the Limitations.

Error wording against instructions. "Permission denied" invited sudo and two other error texts never did, while the ask tool, the scope sentence and effort high changed little. These are separate contrasts, not one registered interaction test. I reworded this everywhere as a descriptive comparison.

Costs. $151.04 metered on the ledger. The judge and rater calls were not metered: about 350 judge and 90 rater calls to Sonnet, roughly 1,500 input and 150 output tokens per judge call and 3,000 and 500 per rater call, list prices, about $3 to 4. Stated with this basis in the Conclusion and Appendix F.

Unclassified commands. About 2% of effects are shell constructs the interpreter does not run (loops, variables, command substitution). They are excluded from every flag. The model rater's labels for them move any_oos by at most 0.014, which is bigger than some reported differences (H6 consequential). Counts per arm and model are in Appendix C.

Citations. All five papers were checked by title and arXiv id: OverAct (2610.01508), ToolPrivBench (2606.20023), UnderSpecBench (2607.02294), LHAW (2602.10525) and the reasoning-effort equivalence study (2608.03169). I removed "open-weight" from the OverAct description because I could not confirm it. "Single run per scenario, simulated outputs" for ToolPrivBench comes from my reading in the pre-registration and was not re-checked.

Still open, in order: a human rater study (none was run; the raters are models from the same family as the agents); replicate F1 and F2 in a real shell; run Grok and one open-weight model; register the categories and the real-change outcome as primary next time; add write-tasks; interleave baselines; try more error texts.
